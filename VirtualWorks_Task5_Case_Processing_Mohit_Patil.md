## 1. Objective

To understand the complete workflow of Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) processing in pharmacovigilance, from receipt and triage of an adverse event report to quality review, medical review, and regulatory submission.

## 2. Introduction

An adverse event report may be received from solicited sources, such as clinical trials and compassionate-use programs, or unsolicited sources, such as spontaneous reports and literature.

An **Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)** is an individual report describing a suspected adverse event associated with a medicinal product.

The date on which a company first receives a report is generally referred to as **Day 0** or the regulatory clock start date.

## 3. Regulatory Reporting Timelines

The tutorial describes the following commonly taught timelines:

- **7 calendar days:** Certain fatal or life-threatening cases.
- **15 calendar days:** Other serious cases.
- **90 days:** Non-serious cases in some reporting frameworks.

**Note:** Regulatory timelines vary according to jurisdiction, product type, and applicable regulations. Always verify current requirements.

## 4. Main Steps in ICSR Case Processing

The case-processing workflow consists of:

1. Triage or case intake.
2. Data entry.
3. Quality review.
4. Medical review.
5. Regulatory submission.

### 4.1 Triage / Case Intake

Triage is the initial assessment of an incoming adverse event report.

Key activities include:

- Confirming whether the report meets the minimum criteria for a valid ICSR.
- Checking the four minimum validity elements:
  - Identifiable patient.
  - Identifiable reporter.
  - Suspected medicinal product.
  - Suspected adverse reaction or event.
- Searching the safety database for potential duplicate reports.
- Prioritizing the case according to seriousness and applicable timelines.
- Creating or booking the case into the safety database.
- Identifying missing information and raising queries.
- Moving the case to the next workflow stage.

Examples of safety databases mentioned in the tutorial include Argus and Rave.

### 4.2 Data Entry

During data entry, the information provided in the source report is entered accurately and completely into the safety database.

Key responsibilities include:

- Entering patient, reporter, product, event, and medical history information.
- Ensuring data are consistent with the source report.
- Coding medical terms using MedDRA where applicable.
- Coding medicinal products using appropriate drug dictionaries such as WHODrug or company dictionaries.
- Preparing a clear case narrative.
- Identifying missing or unclear information and raising queries.

### 4.3 Quality Review

Quality review verifies the accuracy, completeness, and consistency of case data entered into the safety database.

The quality reviewer checks:

- Accuracy and completeness of data entry.
- Consistency of entered information with source documents.
- Correct coding of medical terms and medicinal products.
- Quality and clarity of the case narrative.
- Appropriate documentation of missing information and queries.
- Compliance with applicable processing procedures.

The case may be corrected or returned for clarification before proceeding further.

### 4.4 Medical Review

Medical review is performed by an appropriately qualified medical reviewer.

Key responsibilities include:

- Assessing the clinical information and medical plausibility of the case.
- Reviewing the reported event and its relationship to the suspected drug.
- Evaluating causality or relatedness, as appropriate.
- Reviewing seriousness and relevant clinical details.
- Checking medical coding where necessary.
- Adding medical or company comments when appropriate.
- Identifying additional information required and raising queries.

### 4.5 Regulatory Submission

The submission stage ensures that the case is complete and submitted to the appropriate regulatory authority within the applicable reporting timeline.

Key activities include:

- Performing final completeness and validity checks.
- Confirming the case is ready for submission.
- Checking applicable reporting requirements and timelines.
- Submitting the ICSR through the required reporting system or format.
- Recording submission details and maintaining documentation.
- Managing acknowledgements, rejections, or follow-up actions according to procedure.

## 5. Summary of the Case-Processing Workflow

**Receipt of Report → Triage / Book-in → Data Entry → Quality Review → Medical Review → Regulatory Submission**

Each stage contributes to accurate, complete, timely, and compliant safety reporting.

## 6. Roles and Qualifications Discussed

The tutorial explains that triage, data entry, quality review, and submission roles may be performed by professionals with relevant life-science qualifications, subject to employer requirements and training.

Examples include pharmacy, nursing, dentistry, and other life-science backgrounds.

Freshers may be considered for entry-level data-entry roles by some organizations.

Medical review generally requires an appropriately qualified medical professional, commonly an MBBS or MD, depending on the role and applicable requirements.

Actual eligibility and experience requirements vary by organization and position.
